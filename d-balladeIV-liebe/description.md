# Piano Waterfall (balldeIV) — description

## Pieces
- `web/data/pieces.json` lists the pieces; each has a score `data/<id>.json` and page images. The first entry is the default.
- **Ballade No. 4, coda** (m.168–239, 6/8, 2146 notes): pitch and notation from the PDF, rhythm from a MIDI file. Default tempo 84.
- **Liebestraum No. 3** (whole piece, 6/4, 1890 notes, 88 measure entries, 544 quarter notes): everything from the PDF, because no MIDI of this edition is available. Default tempo 92. See the Liebestraum sections below.
- **Barcarolle, Op. 60** (whole piece, 12/8, 116 bars, 4375 notes of which 110 grace notes): everything from the PDF, a Finale engraving. Default tempo 66 quarters (a dotted-quarter pulse of 44). Marked `localOnly`: the Pages build leaves it out. See the Barcarolle sections below.

## Method (Ballade)
- The PDF is a Sibelius vector export (Opus fonts, no images). `omr/` reads glyphs and paths with PyMuPDF: staves, clefs, key signature, accidentals, noteheads, stems, beams, flags, dots, rests, ties, 8va lines. Pages are analysed in parallel on all CPU cores (about 1 s for 21 pages).
- **Pitch/hand/position/ties** come from the PDF. **Rhythm** comes from the Mutopia MIDI (Joseffy edition), matched head-by-head by pitch and order within the bar. The RH track drifts a constant −30 ticks against the LH, which is corrected per bar from columns the PDF shows as simultaneous. Onsets are snapped to 1/24 beat.
- `web/` is a static app: Canvas waterfall, pre-rendered pages with percentage-positioned highlights, and WebAudio. Microphone audio goes AudioWorklet → MessagePort → Web Worker. The worker does FFT calibration (f0, inharmonicity, partial amplitudes per note), joint non-negative fitting of expected notes, iterative cancellation for unexpected ones, and spectral-flux/amplitude-rise onset detection.

## Liebestraum No. 3: rhythm from the PDF alone
- **Source and extractor.** `sheet-pdf/liebestraum3.pdf` is a Sibelius vector export from mfiles.co.uk (6 pages, 6/4, A♭ major). It differs from the Ballade PDF in ways the extractor now handles: glyphs are reported as private-use codes carrying the Mac Roman byte, noteheads are 17.3 pt (sizes are relative to the page's full size), flat beams are filled rectangles, 8va lines are chains of short strokes (and run between the staves for the left hand), whole-note heads and the double sharp have their own glyphs, and the half and whole rest glyphs are told apart by code. Key signatures are read per staff after the clef and after every double barline (four flats, five sharps at m.32, none at m.43, four flats at m.56). Parsing the Ballade again gives the same notes as before.
- **Rhythm.** `omr/rhythm.py` solves each bar. Events at the same x form a column; a beam search chooses the onset of every column. A column may start where an earlier note ends, at the previous column's time (a note head engraved sideways), or at an unwritten rest (costly). A path is scored by how well one engraving layout explains the gaps between columns (gap proportional to time^0.6, extra room before accidentals, fitted per bar with robust residuals), by a note starting on a multiple of its own value, by dangling or skipped note ends, by overrunning the bar, and by uncovered time on a staff. Tuplets (scale 2/3) are chosen per beam group; a whole-bar rest spans the bar wherever it is centred. `omr/build_pdf.py` runs the bars in parallel (about 30 s), lays the measures on one time axis, merges ties and writes the score. A bar is `normal` (6 quarters), `pickup` (m.1: one quarter, the sum of its notated values) or `free`.
- **Free-time bars.** m.26–28, 29–31, 65–68, 69–72 and m.73 are cadenzas written in small notes with hidden barlines. Each is one engraved row; its columns are spread by x at a fixed speed (four column spacings per quarter note). That speed is an assumption, not a reading of the score. The measure menu names such a row by its printed numbers (for example 26–28).
- **Web app.** Measures carry their own start and length, so the bar lines, the measure menu and the sheet highlights work for a pickup, a 6/4 metre and free rows. The sheet view uses each page's real size.

## Barcarolle: a Finale PDF
- **Source.** `sheet-pdf/barcarolle.pdf` (copied from `materials/PianoSheets/`) is a critical edition by Eric Tran (©2021), 13 US-letter pages, exported from Finale through macOS Quartz. Music glyphs use the Maestro font, which has the same Sonata character layout as Opus, so the glyph table is shared. Text is Playfair Display.
- **What the extractor had to learn** (all switched on by the font or the geometry, so the Sibelius PDFs parse exactly as before):
  - *Barlines* are 0.38 pt, as thin as stems. A thin stroke counts as a barline only when it runs exactly from the top staff line to the bottom one (`staves.py`).
  - *Beams* are open paths with an implicit close; the loader kept only three corners, so every beam was a triangle. The last corner is now kept. Finale ends a stem inside the beam rather than at its outer edge, so a beam may reach 3 pt beyond the stem tip (`primitives.py`, `events.py`).
  - *Augmentation dots* are the plain "." glyph, not Opus's own dot glyph. They attach only just right of a head, so staccato dots above or below are not taken (`events.py`).
  - *Up stems* sit 6.13 pt (per 19.8 pt head) right of the head origin instead of 6.7 (`events.py`).
  - *Ties and slurs* are closed filled outlines whose path starts and ends at the same point; their ends are now the leftmost and rightmost points. Before this, no tie was found (`primitives.py`). 82 tie chains are merged.
  - *Ottavas* are the Maestro "8va" glyph followed by a dashed path. Dashed paths also extend cresc., dim. and calando, so a line is an ottava only with the label at its left end (`marks.py`). Four are found (m.80, 110, 114, 115).
  - *Ossia and footnote staves.* The edition prints source variants on small staves above some systems and music examples in footnotes. Staves spaced under 85% of the page's widest staff spacing are found, and everything inside their band (staff ± 3.5 spaces, plus room for the clef) is dropped before analysis (`small_staves`, `drop_small_staves`). Without this, about 290 variant notes were read into the real bars.
- **Free-time bars** are now those with a *run* of at least eight small notes on one staff, not eight small notes in the bar: trill terminations (m.106) are pairs of grace notes in a metred bar. Only m.110 and m.115 (small-note cadenzas) are free.
- **Grace notes** in metred bars were dropped before. They are now exported (`grace: true`): a run of small notes leads into the next full-size note on its staff (or the barline), one 32nd each, ending where that note starts (`place_graces` in `build_pdf.py`).
- **Anchored solving (`--anchor`).** The bar solver's layout model was tuned on Sibelius spacing; Finale spaces less evenly and offsets the two hands' simultaneous notes by 2–3.5 pt. With `--anchor`, `solve_bar` first fixes the columns of every voice whose notated values fill the bar exactly (one stem direction, one event per column, at least four events: `known_onsets`), and a note tied from earlier in the bar must start where its source ends (`tie_links`, cost 4 otherwise). Only the remaining voices are searched. A bar whose anchored columns would run against x order falls back to the plain search. The Liebestraum is built without `--anchor`: the tie rule alone moves its m.41 the wrong way.

## Barcarolle: trills
- **Reading** (`omr/ornaments.py`). The "tr" sign is glyph 376 in Sonata-layout fonts; the wavy line after it (126) is not needed, because a trill lasts its note's written length with ties. A sign belongs to the nearest staff, and on that staff to the vertically nearest head whose x is within −9…+14 pt (a chord with accidentals pushes its head up to 8 pt right of the sign). A double trill has one sign above the chord and one below, so each goes to the head on its side (m.26, m.84). A sign over a tied continuation moves to the tie chain's first note; if that note already trills, the sign makes its chord a double trill and the chord's newly struck note trills too (m.23: A#4 held, Cx5 struck). All 25 signs are placed.
- **Upper note.** The next letter up, altered by the sign's own small accidental (Maestro "I" = sharp, m.82/83), else by an accidental earlier in the bar on that letter, else by the key signature; the head's ottava applies.
- **Playing** (`expand_trills` in `build_pdf.py`). The trilled note becomes main and upper note alternating over its whole length, an odd number (at least 3) of equal notes about a 32nd long, starting and ending on the main note. The first is an ordinary note (a Learn step); the others are marked `grace` (not required) and all carry `orn: "tr"` and the trilled note's written value `v`. Grace notes printed after a trill (its termination) follow it as before. 380 trill notes in all.

## Validation of the Barcarolle
- **Regression.** Parsing the Ballade and the Liebestraum gives the same events as before (pitch, value, beams, dots, ties, grace), and the Liebestraum build is byte-identical to the previous code's.
- **Self-proving voices.** In 82 bars some voice fills its bar exactly (1285 events). The plain solver, without that knowledge, gets 84.6% of them right (51 bars fully right; it gets 99.7% on the Liebestraum), which is why anchoring is used here.
- **Hold-out test of anchoring.** In the 32 bars where both hands have such a voice, one hand is anchored and the other hand's provable voice is predicted: 96.3% correct (RH anchored, LH tested; 24 of 32 bars fully right) and 97.2% (LH anchored, RH tested; 27 bars), against 90.9% without anchors. In the real build every provable voice is anchored, so those voices are right by construction; this figure estimates the rest.
- **By eye** (with `tools/review.py`): m.1–14, 29–30, 80–83 and 106 against the engraving. m.82 and m.83 were wrong before ties and anchoring (triplets invented in the left hand) and are right now, except one left-hand note of m.83 (C#4 at beat 4.5 instead of 5; its voice mixes stem directions, so it is not anchored).
- **Still to look at.** 29 metred bars leave a stretch of a staff uncovered (some are real: m.1's left hand is empty after its rest), and the solver's cost is high in many bars (m.77–81, 98). On this engraving a low cost does not prove a right reading, so these bars have not been confirmed note by note.
- `tests/browser.mjs` (46 checks) also covers the trills (25 placed, m.82 alternates C#5–D#5, only the first note is a learn step) and the selection: a click jumps, dragging m.4–6 selects them with boxes, rhythm marks and a legend, 8th and 16th notes get different colours and notes outside keep the hand colour, Shift/Ctrl-click, looped playback at tempo 220 never leaves the two selected runs and wraps round, Learn mode cycles through the selected measures only, Esc clears everything. It loads the Barcarolle: 12/8, 116 bars, 13 pages, only m.110 and m.115 free, every full-size note inside its measure, m.1's C#1–C#2 octave, m.82's left hand in plain eighths, the 8va of m.80 sounding E7 while the ossia above it is ignored, grace notes shown but not in the learn steps, playback with sheet highlights.

## Repeated figures (cadenza patterns)
- **What.** In the Liebestraum cadenzas (m.23–31 and m.64–73) a figure is played, then again an octave higher or lower, and again. The app marks each repetition as a cell (A, B, C…) with its own colour, so the structure can be seen at a glance.
- **Finding them (`omr/patterns.py`).** Per hand, the notes of a range are grouped into frames (everything struck at one onset). A chain is a run of cells of L frames where each cell equals the previous one moved by a whole number of octaves (or by 0). A cell counts as a repeat when at least 60% of its frames agree exactly, because the score varies a note now and then (for example m.69: C#7 in cell A, D in B and C). Chains are chosen by the most exactly repeated onsets, then cells that stay inside a bar, then cells starting on a note that comes back, then most onsets covered, then the shortest cell. Leading frames the next cell does not echo are dropped, and a partial cell is added where the neighbouring onsets echo the start or end of a cell. Repeats at other intervals are ignored on purpose: m.65–68 also moves down chromatically in pairs, but the octave cell is the figure a player recognises.
- **Result.** 12 chains and 53 cells: the rising six-note figure and its descent in m.26–28 (both hands), the unchanged alternation of m.29–31 (13 repeats per hand), the bass figure of m.25 and m.64, the 24-note descent in m.65–68 (A, B an octave apart in both hands), and the rising and falling figures of m.69–72. Reading by eye, the cell boundaries are plausible; some are a rotation of the figure (m.69 rises from D4 or from Eb4 equally well).
- **Not covered.** Figures that repeat by contour but not by octave (the arches in m.23, the chromatic sequence of pairs in m.65–68) and echo insertions inside a descent (m.28, where a pair is played twice) are only partly grouped. Notes outside a chain keep their hand colour.
- **Data and display.** `tools/add_patterns.py` (or `export_piece.py --patterns`) writes `patterns` into the score: per chain the hand, shift, cell length and each cell's note ids. `web/js/patterns.js` assigns letters (they continue along a hand inside a patch, A B C then D E F) and colours: six colours, a patch of chains that sound together starts three colours after the previous one, the left hand is a darker shade, and an unchanged repetition alternates two colours and is labelled ×N. `waterfall.js` draws the colour and the letter, `pdfview.js` the bands, and `main.js` the legend and the setting. `tools/show_patterns.py` prints what was found. Tests: `tests/patterns_test.py`, plus the Liebestraum checks in `tests/browser.mjs`.

## Measure selection and rhythm colours (web app)
- **Clicking the sheet.** `pdfview.js` puts a transparent target over every measure (its x range, the system's staves ±14 pt). A press and release on the same measure jumps there (0.4 beat lead-in when stopped, the measure menu shows the clicked bar); a drag across measures selects them; Shift-click selects from the last clicked measure; Ctrl/⌘-click adds or removes one. Pages keep their aspect ratio before their lazy image loads, so the targets are in place.
- **Looping.** A selection is a set of measure numbers, grouped into runs of consecutive measures (`selectionOf` in `score.js`). In Display mode playback jumps from the end of a run to the next (and from the last back to the first, with a quarter-beat lead-in), and only notes of selected measures sound. In Learn mode ← / → and correct notes step only through steps in the selection, round and round. Choosing a measure outside the selection (menu or plain click) ends it, as do Esc and the ✕ on the loop bar.
- **Rhythm colours** (`rhythm.js`). Inside the selection each note is coloured by its written value: 32nd or shorter red, 16th orange, dotted 16th yellow, 8th green, dotted 8th cyan, quarter blue, dotted quarter violet, half magenta, dotted half or longer white, triplets and other tuplets lime, grace notes grey; the left hand is a shade darker. The value is the note's `v` (from the engraving: a tied note keeps its first head's value, a trill note the trilled note's value); the Ballade, built from MIDI, has no `v`, so its sounding length is used and tied or held notes take the nearest class. The sheet gets a mark in the same colour on every head of the selected measures (one per head, so a trill is one mark), the waterfall dims notes outside the selection, and the loop bar lists the values present.
- The Liebestraum score was rebuilt only to add `v`: notes, measures, patterns and pages are otherwise unchanged. `export_piece.py` now keeps a rebuilt piece's place in the menu.

## Piano sound
- Display mode plays recorded grand-piano samples, not a synthesizer. `web/assets/piano/` holds 30 MP3s (A0, then C, D#, F# and A of each octave, up to C8, about 2 MB). `web/js/synth.js` decodes them once at page load and plays each note by resampling the nearest sample (at most a minor third away, so at most ±1.5 semitones of stretch).
- Each note is held for its notated length, then damped with an exponential release (slower in the bass, faster in the treble). Velocity scales both the gain and a low-pass filter, so softer notes sound duller. Pausing, seeking or muting fades out anything still ringing.
- There is one velocity layer and no pedal, so sustained passages are as long as the score says, not as long as a pedalled performance. If the samples fail to load, `synth.js` falls back to the old additive oscillator voice.
- Samples: Salamander Grand Piano V3 by Alexander Holm, licensed CC BY 3.0 (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/>), in the MP3 conversion distributed with Tone.js (<https://tonejs.github.io/audio/salamander/>). Changes: none to the audio; pitches between samples are produced by playback-rate resampling.

## Validation of the Ballade coda (m.168–239, 2146 notes)
- Pitch vs the independent MIDI: 2090 of 2143 PDF notes are identical (97.5%). 44 of 72 bars are identical.
- Remaining differences were inspected visually for m.172–226 and m.234. They are edition differences (the printed sheet contains notes, octaves or accidentals the MIDI lacks) or ornament placement at barlines. The PDF is treated as authoritative.
- Bugs found and fixed through this audit: tie detection (dots, cross-system, wrong chord tone), 8va line owned by the wrong staff, bass notes shifted −12.
- Microphone, synthetic piano audio (different timbre, +6 cent tuning): calibration recovers the tuning exactly; sparse steps 24/24 with no false onsets; dense 5–9 note chords about 85% (octave doubles are inferred from the lower note).
- `tests/browser.mjs`: 31/31 checks for both pieces, 34/34 with `--mic` (the live fake-microphone session advanced 23 steps). 16 of them are Ballade and audio checks: piano-sample loading, note triggering, pause silencing. The tests confirm the audio graph runs; they do not judge how the piano sounds.

## Validation of the Liebestraum
- **Known onsets.** In 65 bars one voice adds up to exactly 6 quarters, so its onsets follow from the notated values alone. The solver is run on the whole bar without that information: 933 of 936 events agree (99.7%) and 64 of 65 bars are exact (`tools/validate_rhythm.py`). The differing bar (m.20) is a flaw of the reference: its stem-direction "voice" mixes two voices. The solver's costs were tuned on this set, so the figure is optimistic, and the set holds the easy bars; the bars with hidden rests have no such proof.
- **Consistency of all bars.** In all 82 normal bars the notes and rests cover both staves from start to end, except m.90 (a run that crosses from the bass staff to the treble staff, so each staff has a gap). No note starts left of a note to its right (displaced heads aside) and every note lies inside its measure (`tools/audit_pdf.py`, and the gap list that `tools/export_piece.py` prints).
- **By eye.** `tools/review.py` draws every note's name and onset on the page. I looked through all 31 systems (pitch and rhythm alignment, clefs, 8va, ties, the triplet in m.58, both cadenzas). That is a plausibility check, not a note-by-note proof. Bugs it exposed and that are fixed: no ottava was applied at all, whole-note chords were missing (m.95, 99), dotted half rests were taken for whole-bar rests, a head engraved sideways split a chord in time, and runs preceded by hidden rests were shifted by half a beat (m.47, 64).
- **Pitch.** 74.8% of all notes belong to the key in force. The bars with the fewest are chromatic harmony in the score, not errors (for example m.41 is F major written with naturals inside five sharps). Range E1–G7.
- `tests/browser.mjs` also loads the Liebestraum: both pieces listed, 88 measure entries, a pickup of one quarter, five free-time rows, every ordinary bar 6 quarters, every note inside its measure, playback and sheet highlights on the A4 page, the repeated figures (found only in the cadenza bars, octave repeats, the m.69 cells A B C, differing colours, legend, sheet bands, the off switch), learn steps for the whole piece.

## Limitations / not done
- **Liebestraum timing has no independent reference** (the MIDI that matches this edition is a paid download). It is a reading of the engraving. Where a voice has hidden rests (for example the left-hand runs in m.47–49) the solver infers them from the layout; a wrong choice would move a run by half a beat.
- Tempo is constant: ritardando, accelerando and fermatas are not modelled (a fermata note lasts its written value). Cadenza speed is an assumption (see above). The tempo slider changes everything at once.
- Hands are staves. A melody written in the bass staff (the dotted cross-staff lines) is shown as left hand. Rolled-chord signs and pedal marks are not modelled; the Liebestraum has no grace notes.
- The Liebestraum PDF and its page images are © Music Files Ltd (free for personal use); keep them out of any public deployment unless the licence allows it.
- The Barcarolle PDF and its page images are © 2021 Eric Tran. The piece is marked `localOnly` in `web/data/pieces.json`, and `scripts/build-pages.mjs` drops its score, page images and menu entry from the published copy (`scripts/check-additional-sites.mjs` counts only public pieces). The source files themselves are in git.
- **Barcarolle timing has no independent reference** either. The ossia variants are not offered, and grace-note timing (one 32nd each, before the beat) and trill speed (32nds, starting on the main note) are conventions, not readings of the score. Trills in the Ballade are not realised (its notes come from the MIDI file).
- **Timing is not independently verified.** 63 notes (about 3%) have no MIDI partner and use interpolated timing. Notated durations are from the PDF with a 1 or 2/3 tuplet scale guessed from beam groups; tied notes are merged.
- m.172, 173, 188–193, 200–214, 218–225 and the m.163–167 cadenza were not all reviewed visually. Only m.168–239 is exported. `omr/timing.py` (an earlier PDF-only solver) is unused by the Ballade build; only its `beam_groups` helper is shared with `omr/rhythm.py`.
- Grace notes and ornaments (trills, arpeggio marks) are shown as plain notes. Only 3 grace notes are flagged in the coda.
- Tested only with synthetic audio, not a real piano. GPU: this machine has no usable GPU driver, so the processing is CPU-parallel only and the waterfall uses Canvas 2D (browser-accelerated).
- The MIDI and any third-party material are used under their own licences; licensing is left to the owner.
