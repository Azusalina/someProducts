Chapter 16

Networking and Internet basics

How computers connect, what a network gives you, and what each box in it does.

What a computer network is

A computer network is a group of computers, peripherals (printers, scanners) and communication devices linked together. It exists for two reasons: to exchange information and to share resources.

LAN: Local Area Network

Covers a small area such as a classroom, an office or one building.

WAN: Wide Area Network

Covers a wide geographical area, such as a city or a country. The Internet is the largest WAN.

Two ways to organise a LAN

In a peer-to-peer network every computer is equal: each one can share files and also use files from others. In a client/server network one powerful computer (the server) holds the shared resources and every other computer (a client) asks it for things.

Wireless connection

A WLAN (wireless LAN) connects devices by radio instead of cable. Two parts are needed:

  • Access point: talks to wireless devices by radio, and also connects to the wired network. It converts between radio signals and digital signals.
  • Wireless NIC (Network Interface Card): the part inside your device that lets it join the WLAN.

  • Lower set-up cost
  • Flexible network layout
  • Can reach outdoor areas

Disadvantages of wireless

  • Higher risk of unauthorised access

  • Limited coverage
  • A large wireless network costs more

Pick the right one

A school with hundreds of users needs speed and reliability: broadband or ISDN. A traveller with a phone has no fixed line: WAP, GPRS or a Wi-Fi hot spot. A remote home with only a phone line: dial-up. Exam questions usually give a user and ask you to match them.

Network hardware

Network Interface Card (NIC)

A NIC is an expansion card that connects a computer to a network. Every NIC has a unique MAC address (media access control address) that identifies that computer on the network. A NIC is described by three things: its connector (BNC or RJ-45), its expansion bus (ISA, PCI, PCMCIA, USB) and its bandwidth (10, 100 or 1000 Mbps; 1 Mb = 1000 Kb here, not 1024).

What a modem does to a signal

Type some bits. The modem turns each bit into a different tone (a fast wave for 1, a slow wave for 0) so the data can travel on an analogue line.

Check yourself

Why is a client/server network more secure than peer-to-peer?

Resources and user accounts are managed in one place (the server), so the administrator can set rules once. In peer-to-peer each user controls their own shared files.

Name three advantages and two disadvantages of a WLAN.

Advantages: lower set-up cost, flexible layout, can extend outdoors. Disadvantages: unstable signal, higher risk of intrusion, slower than wired, limited range.

What is the difference between a hub and a switch?

A hub copies every message to all ports (slow, wasteful). A switch sends the message only to the port of the destination computer (fast).

Bridge or router: which connects a LAN to the Internet?

A router. A bridge joins two networks of the same type.

What do the letters in MODEM stand for in the notes' sense (MO and DEM)?

Modulation (digital to analogue, when sending) and demodulation (analogue to digital, when receiving).

Give two reasons to choose optical fibre, and two reasons not to.

Choose: very high speed, secure, large capacity. Avoid: expensive, difficult to install or change, needs a signal conversion device.

A 600 MB video over a 100 Mbps link: about how long?

600 MB x 8 = 4800 Mb. 4800 / 100 = 48 seconds at best.

Why does satellite broadband use microwaves instead of radio waves?

Radio waves are reflected by the ionosphere. Microwaves pass through it and reach the satellite.
